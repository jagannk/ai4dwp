## Welcome

Microsoft 365 Copilot is built into the tools you already use every day — Outlook, Word, and Excel. It won't replace your judgement, but it can take a lot of the typing and thinking-from-scratch off your plate.

This guide gives you everything you need to start getting real value from Copilot today. No technical knowledge required.

## The One Formula Worth Remembering

Good prompts follow a simple pattern. Think of it as **RATE**:

| Letter | What it means | Example |
|--------|--------------|---------|
| **R** | **Role** — tell Copilot who it's helping | *"I'm a finance analyst..."* |
| **A** | **Action** — say what you want it to do | *"...summarise this report..."* |
| **T** | **Topic/Target** — be specific about the subject | *"...focusing on the Q3 budget variances..."* |
| **E** | **Extra detail** — add tone, length, format | *"...in 5 bullet points, plain English."* |

You don't need all four every time. But the more you give, the better the result.

> **Easy way to remember it:** *"RATE your prompt before you send it."*

## Outlook — Example Prompts

### 1. Drafting a reply to a difficult email

> *"Draft a polite but firm reply to this email. The client is asking for a refund we can't approve. Acknowledge their frustration, explain that our policy doesn't allow refunds after 30 days, and offer a 10% discount on their next order as a goodwill gesture. Keep it under 150 words."*

**Why it works:** You've told Copilot the situation, the constraints, the resolution, and the length. It won't waffle.

---

### 2. Summarising a long email thread before a meeting

> *"Summarise this email thread in 4 bullet points. Focus on: what was agreed, what is still outstanding, and who is responsible for the next action."*

**Why it works:** Asking for a specific structure (agreed / outstanding / owner) means the summary is actually useful in a meeting, not just a word-cloud.

---

### 3. Writing a meeting request

> *"Write a short, friendly meeting invitation to the Accounts team. The meeting is on Thursday 20 August at 2pm for 45 minutes. The purpose is to review the month-end close checklist before the deadline on Friday. Ask them to come prepared with any blockers."*

**Why it works:** All the details are there. Copilot can produce a ready-to-send invite in seconds.

## Word — Example Prompts

### 1. Turning bullet-point notes into a polished update

> *"I have rough bullet-point notes from a client meeting. Rewrite them as a clear, professional summary paragraph suitable for sending to a senior manager. Keep it factual and under 200 words. Here are my notes: [paste your notes]"*

**Why it works:** You bring the facts; Copilot brings the polish. This is one of the highest-value uses of Copilot in Word.

---

### 2. Creating a first draft from a brief

> *"Write a first draft of a one-page internal briefing note. The topic is: why FinBridge is rolling out Microsoft 365 Copilot to all staff. Include: what Copilot is, three benefits for day-to-day work, and a note on data safety. Friendly tone, plain English, short paragraphs."*

**Why it works:** You get a usable starting point in under a minute instead of staring at a blank page.

---

### 3. Improving an existing draft

> *"Review the document I've attached. Identify any sentences that are unclear or overly long and rewrite them. Don't change the meaning or remove any facts. Aim for a reading age of around 14."*

**Why it works:** Asking Copilot to improve — not rewrite from scratch — keeps your voice and content intact while sharpening the language.

## Excel — Example Prompts

### 1. Explaining what a formula does

> *"Explain what this formula does in plain English, as if I've never used Excel before: =IFERROR(VLOOKUP(A2,Sheet2!$A:$C,3,FALSE),"Not found")"*

**Why it works:** No more Googling unfamiliar formulas. Copilot can walk you through them step by step.

---

### 2. Writing a formula from a plain-English description

> *"I have a spreadsheet with sales figures in column B and a target in cell D1. Write a formula for column C that shows 'On target' if the value in column B is equal to or above the target, and 'Below target' if it isn't."*

**Why it works:** You describe what you need in everyday language and Copilot writes the formula. Ideal if you're not confident with IF statements.

---

### 3. Summarising data in a table

> *"Look at the data in this table. Give me a 3-sentence summary of the key trends — which categories are performing best, which are underperforming, and whether the overall picture is improving or declining compared to last month."*

**Why it works:** Copilot can read patterns across large tables and give you an instant narrative, saving time before a review meeting.

## Quick Tips for Better Results

- **Be specific, not vague.** "Write a professional email" is weak. "Write a 100-word professional email declining a meeting request and suggesting an alternative date" is strong.
- **Tell it the audience.** "Explain this to a senior manager who is not a finance expert" gives very different results from "explain this to a new graduate analyst."
- **Iterate.** If the first result isn't right, don't start over — just say: *"That's good, but make it shorter"* or *"Change the tone to be more formal."*
- **Use it to get unstuck.** Even a rough Copilot draft is faster to edit than writing from scratch.

## A Word on Data Safety — Please Read

> **Do not paste confidential client data, personal information, or anything labelled Confidential or Restricted into any AI tool outside of Microsoft 365.**

Microsoft 365 Copilot is approved for use at FinBridge because it works entirely within our secure Microsoft tenant — your data does not leave our environment.

Free tools such as ChatGPT, Google Gemini, or Copilot on the public web are **not** approved for work content. Pasting a client's account details, salary information, or internal financial data into those tools is a data breach — even if it feels harmless in the moment.

This mirrors the data-safety principle you covered on Day 1 of this programme: **think before you share, and know where your data is going.** With Microsoft 365 Copilot, you're safe. With external tools, you're not.

If you're ever unsure whether a tool is approved, ask IT before using it.
